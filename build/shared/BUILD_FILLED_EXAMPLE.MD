## 1. Overview

### 1.1 Purpose

This document defines the preliminary engineering requirements and proposed design direction for a completely custom crankcase.

The crankcase will serve as the main structural housing of the custom engine, supporting the crankshaft, transmission interfaces, lubrication system and other internal components.

The objective is to develop a crankcase designed specifically for the intended custom powertrain rather than simply modifying the original 139QMB crankcase.

### 1.2 Scope

This document covers:

- Crankcase architecture
- Structural design
- Bearing support locations
- Internal component mounting
- Lubrication passages
- Cooling interfaces
- Manufacturing considerations
- Assembly requirements

The detailed design of individual bearings, seals, shafts and fasteners will be documented separately.

### 1.3 Background

The original engine uses a crankcase designed around the factory 139QMB architecture.

The custom project intends to develop a new engine platform with independently engineered dimensions, component interfaces and manufacturing specifications.

The original crankcase will be used as a reference for understanding the existing installation and packaging constraints, not as an unquestioned design specification.

## 4. Design Description

### 4.1 General Design

The proposed crankcase will consist of a precision-machined housing assembly.

The architecture will be developed around the selected crankshaft, cylinder, transmission and lubrication requirements.

The design will prioritise rigidity, dimensional accuracy, serviceability and manufacturing repeatability.

### 4.2 Architecture

Proposed main sections:

1. Crankshaft housing.
2. Cylinder mounting region.
3. Transmission housing interface.
4. Lubrication passages.
5. Bearing support structures.
6. Oil sealing surfaces.
7. Engine mounting interfaces.
8. Cooling-system interfaces.

### 4.3 Design Considerations

Important considerations include:

- Bearing alignment across crankcase sections.
- Adequate wall thickness around bearing housings.
- Thermal expansion of the housing material.
- Oil passage accessibility.
- Machining access.
- Fastener placement.
- Ease of future disassembly.

### 4.4 Alternatives

| Design | Advantages | Disadvantages | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modified original crankcase | Lower initial development effort | Retains original architecture constraints | Not preferred |
| Custom CNC-machined crankcase | High dimensional control and design flexibility | Higher manufacturing cost | Preferred for prototype |
| Custom cast crankcase | Potentially suitable for production | Requires tooling and casting development | Future consideration |
